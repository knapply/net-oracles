# Notes on the data files

The JSON Schema of every file is in `schema/`. This note records how measures were grouped into concepts and concepts into subfamilies, so the grouping can be reviewed and extended.

## Levels

- A **measure** (`measures.json`) is one entry in the survey: a function of a tool, or a published quantity, with its own capability rows, convention records and findings. Measure ids are stable; every other file refers to them.
- A **concept** (`concepts.json`) groups measures. A measure belongs to exactly one concept (`measures.json[].concept`) and carries a short `variant` label that is unique within the concept.
- A concept belongs to one **subfamily** inside one **family** (`concepts.json[].family`, `.subfamily`). The family on a measure (`measures.json[].family`) is the family of the original catalogue and is kept for reference; the browsing family is the one of its concept.
- `aliases.csv` maps each measure id to its concept page and the anchor of its section. It is generated by `scripts/build.py`, and `--check` verifies that every measure appears in exactly one concept page, that every alias resolves to a section of its page, and that no section is orphaned.

## Grouping rules

Measures share a concept when they are:

1. **The same quantity**, computed by different tools or under different names (for example the betweenness functions of NetworkX, igraph, graph-tool, sna and others), including approximate or sampled versions, restrictions to subsets of sources or targets, and two-mode versions that only change the normalization.
2. **An alternative algorithm for the same output** (all-pairs distances by Floyd-Warshall, Johnson or repeated single-source search; a shortest path by Dijkstra, Bellman-Ford, A* or bidirectional search; modularity maximization by Louvain, Leiden, greedy merging, the leading eigenvector or an exact solver; bipartite maximum matching by Hopcroft-Karp or Eppstein).
3. **A normalization, direction, weighting or parameter option** of the same quantity (in-, out- and total degree terms; unweighted, weighted and covariate-interaction versions of a model effect; square-root and logarithmic transforms).
4. **One tool's function for it**: tool-specific entry points, iterators, checkers and "list all" versions of the same computation.

Measures are **not** merged when they compute different quantities, even when they share a name stem: node, edge and group versions of a centrality (betweenness, edge betweenness, group betweenness, flow betweenness, load) are separate concepts; matchings of different objective (maximum weight, minimum weight, maximal) are separate; tests and enumerations of a structure are separate from its measures.

Deliberate coarser groupings, chosen to keep the number of concepts browsable. Each member keeps its own section and anchor, so nothing is lost:

- Model statistics are grouped by term family: the 25 stochastic actor-oriented effect families (rate, popularity, activity, similarity, alter, ego, transitive triplets, reciprocity, and so on) and the exponential-random-graph term families (degree, star and path, triple, shared partner, covariate, bipartite, duration), with the individual effect or term as the variant.
- Block-model, dynamics and belief-propagation state classes of graph-tool are grouped by kind of state.
- Layout algorithms are grouped by method (force-directed, stress, layered, geometric, centrality-focus, spectral and multidimensional scaling).
- Graph file formats are grouped by kind (edge-list and adjacency-list text, matrix and spreadsheet, graph description and exchange, binary and serialization, partition and cover files).
- Deterministic graph generators that differ only by the type of graph are grouped (lattices; complete and complete multipartite graphs; clique composites such as barbell, lollipop, tadpole, windmill, caveman and ring of cliques; circulant and ring graphs; algebraic named graphs).
- Edge sparsification methods that score and filter edges are one concept, and statistical backbone extraction methods are another.

Measures whose only description in the source is a name that does not say what is computed are kept as their own single-measure concepts. Those in the subfamily "Unclassified procedures" of "Graph theory and combinatorics" could not be placed by meaning and are kept apart until their definitions are recorded.

## Subfamilies

Families with more than about 40 concepts are split into subfamilies by the kind of object or task. "Graph theory and combinatorics" has isomorphism and subgraph matching, matching, coloring, independent sets and approximation, covers and dominating sets, cycles, Eulerian paths, Hamiltonian paths and tours, planarity, chordal, perfect and special graph classes, degree sequences and predicates, spanners, contraction and reduction, tree decomposition, triangles and local counts, similarity and link prediction, distances between graphs, geometry and unclassified procedures. "Generators and rewiring" is split by model kind: classic random graphs, degree-sequence models, growth and attachment models, small-world, lattice and geometric models, block models, named and deterministic graphs, random trees and forests, rewiring and randomization. Measures that were listed under "Graph theory and combinatorics" but are model terms, layout methods, sparsifiers, centralities or similar were moved to the family that describes them (their original family is kept on the measure).

## Definitions

`definitions/<family-slug>.json` holds the precise definition of every concept of a family (notation, formulas, variants per measure, edge cases with what each tool does); the procedure, the quality bar and an example are in `definitions/README.md`, the edge-case vocabulary in `definitions/_vocabulary.json`.

## Anchors

The anchor of a measure on its concept page is its id with every character other than letters, digits, underscore, dot and hyphen replaced by a hyphen (for example `twomode:degree` becomes `twomode-degree`). Anchors beginning with `concept-` are reserved for the page's own headings.
