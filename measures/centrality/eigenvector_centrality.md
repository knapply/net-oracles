# Eigenvector centrality {#concept-top}

Family: [Centrality](index.html). Subfamily: Spectral and walk-based. Concept id: `eigenvector_centrality`. 3 measures.

## Notation {#concept-notation}

<div class="table-wrapper"><table class="notation"><thead><tr><th>Symbol</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>\(G=(V,E)\)</td><td>graph with node set \(V\) and edge set \(E\); every edge has a source and a target (the two ends of an undirected edge are interchangeable)</td></tr><tr><td>\(A\)</td><td>adjacency matrix: \(A_{uv}\) is the number (or total weight) of edges from \(u\) to \(v\)</td></tr><tr><td>\(\lambda_1\)</td><td>largest eigenvalue of \(A\)</td></tr><tr><td>\(x\)</td><td>score vector, scaled by a stated rule (maximum 1, unit length, or sum 1)</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Definition {#concept-definition}

Eigenvector centrality of every node, with the principal eigenvalue and a connectedness flag.

<div class="formula-block"><p class="formula-name"><strong>Eigenvector centrality (Bonacich 1972)</strong> <span class="applies">applies to: any</span></p><div class="formula">\[Ax=\lambda_1x\ \ \text{(undirected)},\qquad x_v=\frac1{\lambda_1}\sum_{u}A_{uv}x_u\ \ \text{(directed, in-edges)},\qquad x\ge 0\]</div><p class="formula-where">The principal eigenvector: a node's score is proportional to the sum of the scores of the nodes pointing to it. By the Perron-Frobenius theorem a non-negative eigenvector exists; it is unique up to scale when the graph (strongly) connected components containing \(\lambda_1\) are one.</p></div>

<div class="formula-block"><p class="formula-name"><strong>Power iteration</strong> <span class="applies">applies to: any</span></p><div class="formula">\[x^{(k+1)}=\frac{Ax^{(k)}}{\lVert Ax^{(k)}\rVert},\qquad x^{(0)}=\mathbf 1\]</div><p class="formula-where">Von Mises' method; converges when \(\lambda_1\) is simple and dominant in modulus. It does not converge for periodic (for example bipartite) graphs, where \(-\lambda_1\) is also an eigenvalue.</p></div>

<div class="formula-block"><p class="formula-name"><strong>Two-mode version</strong> <span class="applies">applies to: two_mode, bipartite</span></p><div class="formula">\[BB^{\mathsf T}u=\sigma_1^2u,\qquad B^{\mathsf T}Bv=\sigma_1^2v\]</div><p class="formula-where">Leading singular vectors of the incidence matrix \(B\), each mode scaled to length 1 (Borgatti and Everett 1997).</p></div>
