# Degree {#concept-top}

Family: [Centrality](index.html). Subfamily: Degree and strength. Concept id: `degree`. 3 measures.

## Notation {#concept-notation}

<div class="table-wrapper"><table class="notation"><thead><tr><th>Symbol</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>\(G=(V,E)\)</td><td>graph with node set \(V\) and edge set \(E\); every edge has a source and a target (the two ends of an undirected edge are interchangeable)</td></tr><tr><td>\(n=|V|\)</td><td>number of nodes</td></tr><tr><td>\(k(v)\)</td><td>degree of \(v\): number of edge ends at \(v\) (a self-loop contributes 2 in an undirected graph)</td></tr><tr><td>\(k^{\mathrm{in}}(v),\,k^{\mathrm{out}}(v)\)</td><td>in-degree (edges with target \(v\)) and out-degree (edges with source \(v\)) in a directed graph</td></tr><tr><td>\(A_{vu}\)</td><td>number of edges from \(v\) to \(u\)</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Definition {#concept-definition}

Degree of every node: edge endpoints by direction, loops counted twice.

<div class="formula-block"><p class="formula-name"><strong>Degree (Freeman 1978)</strong> <span class="applies">applies to: undirected, raw</span></p><div class="formula">\[k(v)=\sum_{u\in V}A_{vu}\ \ (+\,A_{vv}\ \text{for an undirected loop, so a loop counts twice})\]</div><p class="formula-where">Number of edge ends at \(v\); parallel edges count separately.</p></div>

<div class="formula-block"><p class="formula-name"><strong>In- and out-degree</strong> <span class="applies">applies to: directed, raw</span></p><div class="formula">\[k^{\mathrm{in}}(v)=\sum_{u}A_{uv},\qquad k^{\mathrm{out}}(v)=\sum_{u}A_{vu}\]</div><p class="formula-where">Edges with target \(v\), and edges with source \(v\). The total degree of a directed graph is \(k^{\mathrm{in}}+k^{\mathrm{out}}\).</p></div>

<div class="formula-block"><p class="formula-name"><strong>Normalized degree</strong> <span class="applies">applies to: normalized, simple</span></p><div class="formula">\[C_D'(v)=\frac{k(v)}{n-1}\]</div><p class="formula-where">Share of the other nodes that \(v\) is joined to; lies in \([0,1]\) for simple graphs only. Needs \(n\ge 2\).</p></div>
