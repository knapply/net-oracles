# Global transitivity {#concept-top}

Family: [Descriptive statistics](index.html). Subfamily: Clustering and transitivity. Concept id: `global_transitivity`. 2 measures.

## Notation {#concept-notation}

<div class="table-wrapper"><table class="notation"><thead><tr><th>Symbol</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>\(G=(V,E)\)</td><td>graph with node set \(V\) and edge set \(E\); every edge has a source and a target; the simple reading merges parallel edges and drops loops</td></tr><tr><td>\(n=|V|\)</td><td>number of nodes</td></tr><tr><td>\(\tau(G)\)</td><td>number of triangles</td></tr><tr><td>\(\theta(G)\)</td><td>number of connected triples: paths of length 2, i.e. \(\sum_i\binom{k_i}{2}\)</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

## Definition {#concept-definition}

Transitivity of the simple undirected reading of the network: closed triples over connected triples.

*Definition partly written:* The sna entry (sna\_transitivity: weak and strong transitivity, rank and correlation forms, directed and weighted clustering conventions) is documented only and was never run; its formulas are not recorded in the sources, so only the closed-triple ratio is written.

<div class="formula-block"><p class="formula-name"><strong>Transitivity (Wasserman and Faust 1994; Newman 2003)</strong> <span class="applies">applies to: undirected, simple</span></p><div class="formula">\[T=\frac{3\,\tau(G)}{\theta(G)}=\frac{\text{closed ordered paths }i\!-\!j\!-\!k}{\text{ordered paths }i\!-\!j\!-\!k}\]</div><p class="formula-where">Fraction of connected triples (two edges sharing a node) that close into a triangle; each triangle contains three connected triples. Defined when \(\theta&gt;0\), i.e. some node has degree at least 2; for directed graphs the neighbourhood is read as undirected.</p></div>
