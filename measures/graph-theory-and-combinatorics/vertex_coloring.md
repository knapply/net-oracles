## Definition {#concept-definition}

A greedy colouring: the colour of every node, from 0, by one of seven node-order strategies.

<div class="formula-block"><p class="formula-name"><strong>Proper colouring</strong> <span class="applies">applies to: undirected</span></p><div class="formula">\[c(u)\neq c(v)\ \ \forall uv\in E,\qquad \omega(G)\le\chi(G)\le\Delta+1\]</div><p class="formula-where">\(\chi\) is the least number of colours; NP-hard to find. A loop makes a proper colouring impossible.</p></div>

<div class="formula-block"><p class="formula-name"><strong>Greedy sequential colouring (Welsh and Powell 1967)</strong> <span class="applies">applies to: undirected</span></p><div class="formula">\[c(\sigma_t)=\min\{k\ge0:\ k\notin\{c(u):\ u\in N(\sigma_t),\ u\ \text{earlier in }\sigma\}\}\]</div><p class="formula-where">Colour nodes in the order \(\sigma\), each with the smallest colour not used by a coloured neighbour; at most \(\Delta+1\) colours. The result depends on \(\sigma\): largest first (decreasing degree), smallest last (Matula and Beck 1983), BFS or DFS order, random order, independent-set peeling.</p></div>

<div class="formula-block"><p class="formula-name"><strong>DSATUR (Brelaz 1979)</strong> <span class="applies">applies to: undirected</span></p><div class="formula">\[\sigma_t=\arg\max_{v\ \text{uncoloured}}\bigl(\ |\{c(u):u\in N(v)\}|,\ \deg v\bigr)\]</div><p class="formula-where">Next node: most distinct neighbour colours (saturation), ties by degree; exact on bipartite graphs.</p></div>

<div class="formula-block"><p class="formula-name"><strong>Equitable colouring (Kierstead et al. 2010)</strong> <span class="applies">applies to: undirected</span></p><div class="formula">\[\bigl||c^{-1}(i)|-|c^{-1}(j)|\bigr|\le1\ \ \forall i,j,\ \text{with }k\ge\Delta+1\text{ colours}\]</div><p class="formula-where">Colour classes differ in size by at most one; always possible with \(\Delta+1\) colours (Hajnal and Szemeredi).</p></div>
